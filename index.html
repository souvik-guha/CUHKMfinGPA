How to read this chart & optional effort model

Solid teal and blue steps use the left 0–4 course-GP axis; dotted lines use the right 0–100% weighted-score axis. These are course grade points, not overall GPA. Shaded envelopes show bounds when coursework is blank, not zero-filled scores. A single guide marks the selected course’s next reachable grade, only when its coursework is fully entered. The two courses share the final-exam scenario shown in the readout; their saved exams change only when you move the selected course’s exam control.

Optional effort is illustrative, not a prediction: hours = H × (final % / 100)p. H is the chosen hours at a 100% final; the purple dashed curve uses normalized effort (hours / H × 100%) on the right axis. It does not predict GP or account for ability or preparation. Leave hours blank if not estimated. Percentage cutoffs are the existing slide-based / extrapolated assumptions.

How the cutoffs work

Both templates use the same letter-grade cutoffs and the same CUHK grade-point conversion. Only the component weights differ by course.

Where the cutoffs come from

The IPA 6112 slide gives the course-score cutoff for each grade from A down to C+. Its other two columns translate those cutoffs into exam terms, assuming full marks on the non-exam 45%.

  1. Exam (MT + Final) = (course cutoff − 45) ÷ 55. For a B: (80 − 45) ÷ 55 = 63.6%, shown rounded as 64%.
  2. Midterm Score = that unrounded exam percentage × 30. For a B: 63.64% × 30 = 19.1.
  3. All 12 derived numbers on the slide come out exactly from these two formulas. Two rival readings fail: computing the midterm from the rounded exam % gives 24.6 for A− (slide says 24.5), and ignoring the 45% gives 28.5 for A (slide says 27.3). So the Course Score column is the input the professor set, which makes it the grade cutoff.

Filling in grades the slide leaves out

The slide stops at C+. The cutoffs step down by a fixed number of points per sub-grade: 5 points from A to B, then 10 points from B to C+. The step size does not follow the grade-point gaps (A− to B+ is 0.4 GP and B+ to B is 0.3 GP, yet both are 5 points).

Two models were tested by hiding each cutoff and predicting it from the two above it. "Same step per sub-grade" predicted B+, B and C+ exactly. "Straight line in grade points" missed every one, by 1.2 to 3.3 points. Both miss B− because that is where the step widens from 5 to 10. Continuing the 10-point step gives C 50, C− 40, D+ 30, D 20.

Confidence: A to C+ come straight from the slide. C and below are the best-fitting extrapolation, not published numbers, so edit them below if a course gives its own.
